    Gravel goes on top, not on its own

    A gravel drive needs a compacted sub-base of MOT Type 1, usually 10–15 cm deep, with a membrane under it on soft ground. The gravel is only the top 5 cm. Laying 10 cm of gravel straight onto soil gives you ruts and a beach underfoot.

    Which gravel?

    Use angular gravel of 14–20 mm. It locks together under tyres. Rounded pea shingle rolls about, gets trapped in tyre treads and ends up on the road. Small gravel (10 mm) is fine for paths but tends to stick in tyres on a drive.

    Planning permission

    In England, a new or replacement front drive over 5 m² needs planning permission unless it's permeable or drains to a lawn or border. Loose gravel is permeable, which is one reason it's popular.

    Questions people ask

    How deep should driveway gravel be?

    5 cm on top of a compacted sub-base.

    How many tonnes of gravel for a double drive?

    For 6 × 6 m at 5 cm, about 3.17 t.

    Does a gravel drive need edging?

    Yes. Timber, brick or steel edging stops it spreading onto lawns and pavements.

    Before you order

    • A bulk bag is roughly 90 cm square. It's dropped at the kerb by a crane lorry, so you need somewhere to put it, and it won't go through a side gate.
    • One bulk bag is about 7 or 8 wheelbarrow loads. Budget an hour per bag if you're barrowing it to the back garden.
    • Lay a permeable membrane first. Gravel straight onto soil sinks and grows weeds within a season.
    • Angular gravel (crushed stone) locks together and stays put on a drive. Rounded pea shingle rolls about and gets kicked onto the lawn.
